## Mistake restated
Competitors show a bare balance ("US$5 left") with no translation into what the user actually needs to know: how many minutes this running session has left at the current burn rate, and what the all-in price of the session will be (compute + storage + egress + add-ons). Balance is a number, not a plan. Opacity is profitable — float sits in the wallet and the effective $/hour is never stated — so it persists.

## Strlix solution (concrete UX + IA)
Rule: **never display currency without time, and never display time without the rate that produced it.**

1. **Runtime chip in the phone status bar** (inside the device frame, top-right, next to battery): `2h14m · $0.41/h`. Tap expands a sheet, not a redirect to a billing page.
2. **Burn sheet** (bottom sheet over the phone, 3 rows):
   - *Running total* `$0.37` since 14:02 — ticking every 10s from a server-authoritative meter.
   - *Rate breakdown* line items: compute tier `$0.32/h`, storage 8GB `$0.06/h`, egress est. `$0.03/h`. Sum must equal the headline rate; no "other".
   - *Runs until* `16:41` with the wall-clock time, not just a duration. Below: "Idle at 5 min → $0.09/h (paused compute)".
3. **Pre-flight price card** on every Launch button: before the instance boots, a one-tap card states "Start: $0.41/h · your balance funds 12h04m · auto-stop at 2h (editable)". Launch is disabled until a stop-rule is chosen: *auto-stop at N hours*, *stop at $X spend*, or *run until I stop* (only if balance > 2h).
4. **Spend cap, not balance warning.** The banner at low balance is replaced by a cap-aware timeline: a thin bar under the status chip that fills toward the chosen cap; at 80% the in-phone AI agent surfaces a notification *in the Android notification shade* ("Session stops in 24m. Extend +1h for $0.41?") with Extend / Snapshot & stop / Let it stop.
5. **Graceful stop, never a hard cut.** At T-120s the phone shows a system-style dialog; at T-0 Strlix snapshots disk, stops compute, and keeps storage-only billing ($0.06/h) with the exact resume cost shown on the device card.
6. **Receipts IA:** `/wallet` has Ledger (per-session receipt: duration, rate, line items, snapshot cost) and Rates (a static table of every SKU's $/hour). Any number in the UI links to its row in Rates.
7. **AI inside the phone:** the agent answers "how much have I spent today" from the meter API and can set caps via tool call — it never invents a price; it renders the same burn-sheet component.

## Android 7+ / graceful degradation notes
- Chip and sheet are plain DOM/CSS (flex, no container queries, no `Intl.NumberFormat` options beyond currency); target ES2017 build with a legacy bundle for Chrome 53/WebView 5x.
- No WebSocket? Meter falls back to `fetch` polling at 10s; interpolation keeps the ticker smooth.
- Tab hidden / device sleeps: on `visibilitychange` resume, re-fetch authoritative `accruedCents` rather than trusting the local tick.
- Cap enforcement is server-side, so an offline client never overspends; on reconnect the client reconciles and shows "stopped at 16:41, $0.82".
- Reduced motion / 2G: ticker updates at 30s instead of 10s; no animated fill bar.

## Acceptance criteria
- No screen in the app displays a balance or warning without an adjacent remaining-runtime value and the $/h that produced it.
- Launch is impossible without an explicit stop-rule; the pre-flight card's rate equals the sum of its line items and equals the post-session receipt total (±1 cent rounding).
- Killing the browser tab mid-session still stops compute at the cap, verified in `billing.spec.ts`.
- On Android 7 WebView with WebSocket blocked, the runtime chip still updates within 10s and the burn sheet opens.
- Session end produces a receipt with duration, line items, snapshot cost, and resume price before the user leaves the page.
